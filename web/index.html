Route an utterance onto the graph

One line per question, in whatever words a learner would actually use. Each line runs a beam search: Jev picks the strand, then the concept inside it, and the two probabilities are combined as a geometric mean. Several lines run in parallel.

presets

Sit a concept's test

Answer in your own words — you don't have to pick an option, and you don't have to spell it right. Nothing is revealed until you commit. At the end, Jev reads the whole sitting at once and says whether you have the concept.

Diagnose an answer

Pick a concept, then answer one of its authored questions however you like — typed in full sentences, hedged, misspelt. The graph can only diagnose pre-tagged multiple-choice picks today; this reads free text against the same documented misconceptions, and asks five things in a single request.

try

Audit the graph against itself

Every prerequisite edge was hand-written with a justification, and every wrong option is supposed to detect a named misconception. Nobody has re-read all of them. Jev checks each one and ranks what it finds worst-first.
not checked here whether an answer key is arithmetically right — Jev is documented as not a calculator, and asking it anyway produced three false alarms on correct keys. That check belongs in validate_kg.py.

Jev against the lookup it replaces

The baseline is what /kg find does today: token overlap against node titles and descriptions. It is instant and free, and it is the right tool when a learner already knows the technical word. The question is what happens when they don't.
